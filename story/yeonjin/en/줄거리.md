Plot Summary (Combined Version)

X-Plan was a game development company enjoying stable growth.
However, a massive service outage one day plunges the company into a crisis of confidence.
Dong-gun, the Chief Technology Officer, is at the forefront of problem-solving, while Yeon-jin, a marketing and communication strategist, insists on establishing accountability before issuing apologies.
Dong-gun chooses to step forward as the technical leader for the first time, and the crisis is managed.
This incident forges a relationship between them based on trust, rather than fleeting emotion.

Afterward, X-Plan starts a new game project.
The process, from planning and design to development, testing, and launch, unfolds in short, dense episodes.
Dong-gun emphasizes stability and structure, while Yeon-jin prioritizes user experience and long-term trust, leading to clashes. However, their judgments always point in the same direction.
By cutting off promises they can't keep and accepting the decision to postpone the launch, the project concludes with a quiet but unwavering success.
Through the time they endure together, the two naturally continue their relationship, without ever formally declaring it as "dating".
During this time, Yeon-jin has no intention of leaving X-Plan and is a colleague and companion who deeply empathizes with Dong-gun's beliefs.

Just before launch, **compensation logic errors** and **suspicious payment activity** are discovered during QA.
The compensation is skewed toward one side under specific conditions, and duplicate payments are possible during the retry flow.
Within the team, there's a clash between the pragmatic decision to "block payments first" and the assessment that "trust will be shattered if compensation fairness collapses."
Yeon-jin doesn't dictate a technical solution but suggests first establishing **a standard that preserves trust**.
They set a principle of immediately blocking payments if duplicate possibilities are detected and delaying compensation if fairness criteria are exceeded.
Based on that standard, Dong-gun persuades Chan-woo to address both risks together, and ultimately, a schedule postponement is decided.

However, an external event intervenes.
Yeon-jin's father suddenly passes away, and a will is delivered by a lawyer after the funeral.
Her father bequeathed her 30% of PlantHub's shares, but there was a condition:
**If she doesn't return within three months, the shares will automatically be transferred to Chae Dong-soo.**
Yeon-jin's past is revealed as minimally as possible from her perspective.
PlantHub wasn't a place she wanted to leave; it was a place she had no choice but to leave.
Her older brother, Chae Dong-soo, was a pragmatist who chose performance and expansion, a direction Yeon-jin couldn't accept.
Chae Dong-soo pressures her. "Father gave you one last chance. Return within three months, or give it up forever."

Yeon-jin doesn't explain everything to Dong-gun.
Instead of saying she's returning to PlantHub, she resigns from X-Plan, citing studying abroad as the reason, and breaks up with Dong-gun.
Dong-gun asks for the reason, but Yeon-jin only replies, "I can't tell you right now."
There are no apologies, no promises.
The breakup isn't a matter of emotions but the result of lost options.

After Yeon-jin leaves, Dong-gun's life crumbles.
CEO Chan-woo witnesses Dong-gun's condition and says, "I need you, but you need to rest now. I'll protect the company."
PM Han Do-yoon witnesses Dong-gun's daily collapse most closely and tries to cushion it with humor but fails.
Eventually, Han Do-yoon books a flight to Thailand for him, and Dong-gun temporarily puts the company aside and leaves for Thailand.
There, he meets Rin, an early member of X-Plan.
Rin is someone who chose freedom by leaving the company and Dong-gun.
Rin says, "I didn't dislike the structure you created. I couldn't stand watching you postpone choices within it."
"You've always failed to hold onto people who leave. Because you didn't choose first."
In their brief reunion, Dong-gun realizes:
He's always failed to hold onto people who leave, and he's been someone who postpones decisions at every moment of choice.
The realization that Rin and Yeon-jin left for the same reason hits him.

While Dong-gun is away, cracks appear within X-Plan.
CTO Kang Min-seok discovers an anomaly: PlantHub released features similar to X-Plan's first.
With Dong-gun absent, the code review process collapses, exposing security vulnerabilities.
Along with suspicions of technology leaks, PlantHub's attempted merger and acquisition becomes a serious threat.
Han Do-yoon relays the internal atmosphere of unrest to Chan-woo. "The team members are anxious. Someone needs to make a decision."
CEO Chan-woo receives Kang Min-seok's report and recognizes this crisis not as individual betrayal but as a structural problem.
Early X-Plan investor Lee Joon-ho advises Chan-woo, "In this situation, you should meet Jung Hae-won. He's someone who designs structures."
Introduced by Lee Joon-ho, Chan-woo joins hands with strategic investor Jung Hae-won and designs an alternative that allows the company to survive without selling it.
The hostile takeover is transformed into a different path: long-term structural investment.

Meanwhile, Yoon Se-ra, the head of PlantHub's strategic planning department, appears before Yeon-jin, who is studying abroad.
Yoon Se-ra is skeptical of Chae Dong-soo's aggressive M&A approach.
She doesn't try to persuade.
Without mentioning emotions or family, she only conveys numbers, structure, and the consequences after Yeon-jin left.
"Chae Dong-soo is trying to hostilely acquire X-Plan. You're the only one who can put on the brakes internally."
Yeon-jin makes a choice.
Not to return, but to go stop Chae Dong-soo's hostile takeover of X-Plan.
She sets conditions and returns to PlantHub.
She secures the title of director of a PlantHub subsidiary and the negotiation rights for the acquisition conditions.
Yeon-jin's intention is clear: as a director of PlantHub, to design conditions that are favorable to X-Plan.

PlantHub's merger and acquisition proposal is formalized, and internal divisions arise within X-Plan.
The finance/management team prioritizes survival and supports it, while the technology/planning team prioritizes independence and opposes it.
The internal divisions become concrete through Han Do-yoon, and at Chan-woo's request, Dong-gun urgently returns from Thailand.

The first negotiation table is held.
On the PlantHub side sit Chae Dong-soo (CEO), Yoon Se-ra (Head of Strategic Planning), Yeon-jin (Subsidiary Director), and Park Jae-hyun (CFO).
On the X-Plan side face Chan-woo (CEO), Dong-gun (CTO), Han Do-yoon (PM), and Jung Hae-won (External Investor).
Yeon-jin appears as PlantHub's negotiation representative, and a reunion with Dong-gun occurs.
There are no explanations, no emotional outbursts.
Only tension exists.
At the negotiation table, Yeon-jin begins to propose conditions that are favorable to X-Plan.

Afterward, the truth is revealed one by one.
The reality of the technology leak, Yeon-jin's role, the weight of the choices she had to bear.
Yeon-jin wasn't a traitor, and Dong-gun wasn't someone who was abandoned either.
They were simply standing before the same question in different positions.

The merger and acquisition crisis is resolved by Chan-woo and Jung Hae-won's design.
X-Plan is preserved, and PlantHub doesn't collapse either.
After all choices are made, Dong-gun and Yeon-jin organize their lives in their respective places.

In the ending, the two choose the same direction separately.
They either book a flight to Canada or briefly meet at the departure hall of the airport.
Whether they'll be together again or go their separate ways isn't stated.
However, what's clear is that,
the two people who chose responsibility over love,
have now reached a position where they can look in the same direction.

One-line summary of the core of the plot:

This story isn't about the reunion of separated lovers,
but about the process of two people who lost their right to choose regaining their ability to choose.
